💰 Financial Aid Visual Breakdown

When financial aid packages arrive (Jan–Mar), they mix three very different types of money together. Here is how to read them:

1. Free Money (Never Repaid)

Scholarships & Grants

Reduces your out-of-pocket cost directly. Usually merit-based (scholarships) or need/institutional (grants).

  • High School Guidance Office Ask your counselor early for their list of local & community scholarships.
  • Departmental & Sibling Scholarships Check the college's academic department web pages directly, and always ask if the school offers a Sibling Scholarship.
  • Institutional Grants Free aid awarded directly by the college itself in your financial aid package.
  • Federal Pell Grant & NYS TAP Unlocked by completing the FAFSA and NYS TAP starting Oct 1.
2. Earned Money (Paycheck)

Federal Work-Study

Offered to eligible students to work a part-time job on campus and earn a regular paycheck.

  • How It Works Students apply for eligible campus jobs and get paid as they work hours during the semester.
⚠️ Parent Watch-Out: Work-Study is NOT applied directly to your tuition bill upfront! Even if a college lists "$2,500 Work-Study" in your award letter, the student earns that as spending/living money via a paycheck during the year.
3. Borrowed Money (Repaid + Interest)

Federal Student Loans

Loans must be paid back, and the difference between Subsidized and Unsubsidized saves thousands in interest:

✅ Subsidized Loan

No interest while in school. Interest only starts accruing after graduation. Always accept this before unsubsidized loans if borrowing.

⚠️ Unsubsidized Loan

Accrues interest immediately from day one and throughout all four years of college.

🔄 How the FAFSA "Contributor" Process Works

You must complete the FAFSA every year. Parents are now called "Contributors" and have their own separate login.

1
Both Create FSA IDs Early (studentaid.gov)
Student and Parent each need their own separate FSA ID. Do this early—SSA verification takes days.
2
Student Starts the Form & Invites Parent
Make sure the student is listed as the applicant (not the parent). The student enters parent info to send an invite.
3
Parent Accepts "Contributor" Email Invite
Parent receives an email invite, logs in with their own FSA ID, and completes the parent financial section.
4
Verify BOTH Signatures Went Through
Double-check that both the student and parent signed and that the submission status reads complete.
📞 Stuck on FAFSA or FSA ID verification? Ask for help: 1-800-4-FED-AID (1-800-433-3243)

🎯 Admissions Strategy & Insider Tips

Key definitions and strategies from counselor meetings to strengthen applications and avoid common traps.

Non-Binding (Flexible) Typical Deadline: Nov 1 – Nov 15

⚡ Early Action (EA)

Students apply early in the fall and receive an admission decision early (usually Dec–Jan), without any obligation to attend.

  • Can apply to multiple schools Students are free to apply Early Action to multiple colleges (unless a school specifies "Restrictive/Single-Choice Early Action").
  • Compare Financial Aid Offers until May 1 You do not have to commit until the May 1st deadline, allowing families to compare financial aid packages across all accepted schools.
Binding Commitment Typical Deadline: Nov 1 – Nov 15

🔒 Early Decision (ED)

A binding contract signed by the student, parent, and high school counselor: if accepted, the student must attend and withdraw all other college applications.

  • Only ONE Early Decision school allowed Only use Early Decision if a college is the student's 100% undisputed first choice.
  • Financial Aid Caution for Parents Because you commit before seeing offers from other colleges, you cannot easily compare financial aid packages—only apply ED if the school's Net Price Calculator fits your family's budget.
📊

The "Common Data Set" Lookup Trick

Want to see a college's real admission statistics, percentage of students applying vs. admitted, what they value most, and exact test score percentiles? Search the college's name plus "Common Data Set".

✉️

Show "Demonstrated Interest" (Especially Reach Schools)

Colleges track whether a student genuinely wants to attend. Have the student email universities thank-you notes after campus visits, college fairs, virtual sessions, or rep visits—especially if it is a reach school.

📝

Test-Optional Strategy

Do not submit SAT/ACT scores if test scores are optional unless the score strengthens the application. Check the school's Common Data Set first to see if your student's score is at or above their admitted 50th percentile.

✍️

College Essay: Show Personal Growth (≤ 650 Words)

Admissions officers already have the transcript and activity list. The goal of the Common App essay (maximum of 650 words) is to show how the student has grown as a person through reflection and self-awareness.

🗣️

Take the Optional Interview (When It Fits)

Depending on the student, take the interview option if it is available. It's a great way for conversational students to add personality to their file and reinforce demonstrated interest.

📧

High School Email Deactivation Trap

Make note of when the student's high school email is deactivated after graduation! Never tie FAFSA (`studentaid.gov`), Common App, or college housing portals solely to a school email address that will shut off in the summer.

🖥️

Complete Each School's Post-Common App Portal

Submitting on the Common App is often not the final step. Many colleges email credentials for their own additional applicant portal that must be set up and completed to submit supplemental items and track missing documents.

⏰

Transcripts & Recommendations: Your Deadline vs. Theirs

Remind your high school guidance office and teachers early! Make sure transcripts and letters of recommendation are sent by your earliest application deadline (especially Early Action/Decision), not the high school's general internal schedule.
